# Ticket: Milestone 2 — Leaderboard Page and Statistics Presentation Expansion

- **Conceptual assignee:** Developer 1
- **Implementation owner:** Chandler Lovely (GitHub: ChanMan64)

## Goal

Deliver a polished public Leaderboard experience that expands the working
Milestone 1 application. It must be a genuine feature, not just a renamed
Statistics screen. Select its information architecture only after investigating
the current Statistics readers and presentation.

## Main Success Scenario

1. A visitor opens the public Leaderboard and immediately understands the
   selected ranking, relevant data window, and how to interpret the results.
2. The visitor can move among useful supported categories or views, compare
   meaningful statistics, and navigate to public Player or Unit information.
3. The application derives displayed values from authoritative existing
   statistics semantics and preserves public identity boundaries.

## Context

The repository already has a public `/statistics` discovery surface and
query-time Statistics readers/presentations. The Statistics module currently
provides Ranker, Commander, General, Event Battle, Direct/Organizational/Average
Unit Performance, and Player+Unit attendance. Its shared windows are trailing
14-day (default), trailing 30-day, and all-time. Existing readers derive from
effective finalized Audits and canonical Event times, preserve raw
ratio-numerator/denominator semantics and zero-denominator states, and partition
applicable results by Unit type.

The existing Ranker result includes Player K/D/A totals, per-Audit-appearance
averages, K/D and (K+A)/D ratio states, Audit appearances, distinct Events, and
Unit-type partitions. Commander, General, and Unit readers expose additional
battle, command, organizational, and Unit result metrics. These are discovery
inputs, not a predetermined page specification. Reinspect the current branch
when implementation begins; repository truth wins.

### Discovery findings (2026-10-31)

Inspected on `feat/m2-leaderboard` (base `3c6c7af`): `MODULE.md`, `index.ts`,
`windows.ts`, `ratios.ts`, `ranker.ts`, `commander.ts`, `general.ts`,
`unit.ts`, `event-battle.ts`, the three presentation files, the window
selector, and their tests; `/statistics`, `/players/[id]`, `/units/[unitId]`
and shared `src/app/presentation.tsx`; the Audits → Statistics source and its
contract; the Players → Statistics source; and the demo dataset
(`npm run demo:reset`: 29 FINAL Audits, 132 Players, 10 Events).

- **Readers.** Ranker, Commander, and General are already population-wide
  (one call returns every Player), so they directly support rankings. Unit
  performance readers take a single `unitId`; a Unit ranking via them would
  repeat the full Event-time and Audit source read once per Unit.
- **Identity.** Ranker/Commander/General DTOs carry only the stable game
  Player ID. The approved Audits source already carries each result's public
  name, and the Event Battle reader resolves `publicName?.trim() || gamePlayerId`
  from it; the Leaderboard reuses exactly that fallback. Player routes are
  `/players/[gamePlayerId]`. Units have no public key other than the
  identifier already used by the public `/units/[unitId]` route.
- **Data shape (demo).** 14-day: 37 Ranker players (22 Regular, 9 Rifles,
  5 Cavalry, 1 Artillery), 9 commanders, 9 generals; all-time: 58 / 18 / 23.
  Most players have one Audit appearance; 9 of 37 14-day Ranker rows have zero
  deaths; 102 Audit Player results have no public name.
- **Presentation.** `/statistics` is a thin directory whose "Categories"
  block is a placeholder empty state. Finite ratios render as raw floats
  (e.g. `2.066666666666667`) on Player/Unit pages, while the Event Battle page
  already rounds to two decimals.

### Information-architecture recommendation (selected)

- **Route.** A dedicated public `/leaderboard`, linked from the header (the
  "Leaderboards — coming soon" placeholder becomes a real link) and featured
  on `/statistics`. `/statistics` stays the records directory; it is not
  renamed, so the Leaderboard is a genuine new surface rather than a
  relabeled one.
- **Boards.** Players (Ranker), Commanders, Generals, Units (Direct
  performance). Each answers a real community question: who are the most
  effective fighters in each branch; whose companies perform under their
  command; who leads the largest formations; which regiments field the best
  results.
- **Branch partitions.** Players, Commanders, and Units are ranked separately
  for Regular, Rifles, Cavalry, and Artillery, preserving the readers'
  partitions — an artillery crew's kills are not comparable to line infantry.
  No cross-branch total is shown. Generals are ranked on command scope (which
  the reader reports across branches) with per-branch Unit counts as columns.
- **Windows.** The shared 14-day (default), 30-day, and all-time windows, via
  the existing window selector.
- **Columns and sorting.** Every metric column sorts (descending first, toggle
  to ascending). Defaults: Players by Kills (volume is robust when most
  players have a single Audit); Commanders by battles commanded; Units by
  pooled K/D (size-normalized and meaningful across many-player Audits);
  Generals by qualifying commands. Ties share a rank (1, 2, 2, 4) with a
  stable name tie-break.
- **Ratios.** Shown pooled exactly as the readers derive them (total
  numerator ÷ total denominator), rounded to two decimals for display with the
  raw `numerator ÷ denominator` available on hover. Zero-death rows keep the
  approved `N K` / `N K+A` form and, when the numerator is positive, sort above
  every finite ratio; `0 ÷ 0` sorts last.
- **Omitted (with reason).** Organizational and Average Unit performance (they
  pool or equal-weight whole subtrees, so ranking a nation against a company
  would mislead); distinct Events on the per-branch Player board (the reader
  counts it across branches); General per-branch combat totals (left on the
  Player page); minimum-appearance qualifiers (with most players at one Audit,
  a threshold would hide nearly everyone — the Audits column is shown beside
  every ratio instead); attendance (a per-Unit obligation measure, not a
  ranking).

### Candidate metrics to evaluate (not mandatory)

- **Player:** K/D/A, K/D or (K+A)/D, appearances, distinct Events, per-appearance
  averages, and Unit-type partitions.
- **Commander:** battles commanded, K/D/A, Tickets, Flag Captures/Losses,
  Stars, average player count, ratios, and Unit-type partitions.
- **Unit:** Direct, Organizational, or Average performance; Tickets, Flag
  Captures/Losses, Stars, K/D/A, ratios, and battle/Event counts where
  supported.
- **General/command group:** qualifying command observations, Events,
  subordinate atomic-unit counts, and type-specific combat/performance totals.

Choose a useful subset from repository truth. Do not imply that different
populations or time windows are directly comparable when they are not.

### Implementation owner's design note for the lead (proposals, not implemented)

From Chandler Lovely, 2026-10-31:

> The leaderboard currently looks a little too default. After reviewing other
> games' leaderboards, I see two ways to make it more visually appealing:
> let users upload their own custom avatars, and add rank emblems based on
> percentile standing.

Governance context for whoever picks these up: neither fits inside this
ticket's authorized scope, so each needs its own proposed ticket and approval.

- **Custom avatars** need new persistence (a stored image reference), an
  upload/storage path, upload authorization and validation, and a decision on
  whether an avatar belongs to the website `User` or the game `Player`
  (account-to-Player linking is currently undecided and outside MVP scope).
  That is a schema and ownership change (RED).
- **Percentile rank emblems** are a new derived metric. The Statistics
  `MODULE.md` currently excludes "percentile/ELO/rating" analytics, so this
  needs approval, plus decisions on the population (per branch and window),
  a minimum sample size (most demo players have a single Audit, so percentiles
  would be unstable), and tie handling. Once those semantics are approved, a
  presentation-only version could live in Statistics without persistence.
