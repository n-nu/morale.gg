## Established Requirements

The following are established product requirements for the architecture
work. They replace the former child-owner model.

### Identity and RootUnit Boundary

- Auth.js `User` represents website identity.
- User and Player remain separate concepts.
- Authentication alone grants no management authority.
- `Unit` remains the basic organizational object.
- A Unit may be designated as a `RootUnit`.
- MVP supports only ordinary Unit and RootUnit; do not generalize a
  website/game-wide meta-root.
- A RootUnit is the highest Unit of one independently managed organization.
- A RootUnit may represent any top-level organization, not specifically a
  nation.
- Every Unit belongs to exactly one RootUnit tree, directly or through its
  ancestry.
- Authority and organization-scoped configuration must never cross RootUnit
  boundaries.
- The RootUnit is the future boundary for organization-wide configuration
  such as ranks and medals. Those features are non-MVP.

### RootUnit Bootstrap and Ownership

- RootUnits are created or designated manually by website/system
  administrators.
- The intended RootUnit owner must authenticate at least once so an Auth.js
  User exists before assignment.
- The website administrator assigns that existing User as RootUnit owner and
  RootUnit Commander.
- RootUnit ownership transfer may be performed only by a website
  administrator.
- Delegated users cannot transfer RootUnit ownership.
- RootUnit owner and RootUnit Commander are the same relationship in MVP.

### Commander

- Every Unit has exactly one Commander.
- A Commander is the local owner/formal leader of that Unit.
- Commander status and operational permissions are separate concepts.
- Commander status alone does not grant any operational permission.
- Commander-only approval workflows are future behavior and are not part of
  MVP implementation.
- An ordinary authorized user cannot remove or replace a Commander.
- A descendant Unit's Commander may be replaced only by a User who is both the
  Commander of an ancestor Unit and authorized by applicable
  `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS` authority covering the target Unit.
- `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS` authority alone does not permit Commander
  replacement.
- When a child Unit is created, an eligible ancestor Commander may establish
  its local leadership by designating an already-authenticated Auth.js User,
  subject to the required authority above.

### Unit Hierarchy

- Unit hierarchy has arbitrary depth. Organizational types such as
  Nation -> Regiment -> Battalion -> Company are not enforced.
- Units may be created and moved within their RootUnit tree according to
  authorization.
- Unit creation and initial Commander assignment must occur atomically.
- A valid Unit must never persist without exactly one Commander.
- Moving a Unit moves its descendants with it.
- Inherited authority from former ancestors stops applying after a move.
- Inherited authority from new ancestors begins applying after a move.
- Direct assignments on the moved Unit remain attached to that Unit.
- Authority never propagates upward and never crosses RootUnit boundaries.

### Authorized Users and Permissions

- Users may be authorized at specific Units.
- An `AuthorizedUserMembership` associates a User with a Unit and one local
  authority level; separate `PermissionGrant` records attach permissions and
  applicable scope or structural-anchor authority to that membership.
- Permissions are additive.
- A User may have multiple independent memberships and grants at different
  Units.
- Removing one membership or grant removes only authority derived from that
  membership or grant.
- Initial MVP permissions are:
  - `MANAGE_UNIT`: edit ordinary Unit metadata.
  - `MANAGE_STRUCTURE`: control descendant structure from a structural anchor;
    it does not use the ordinary scope model.
  - `MANAGE_ROSTER`: administer roster/player membership for the applicable
    Unit scope.
  - `REQUEST_EVENT_PARTICIPATION`: request Event participation for an
    applicable Unit.
  - `MANAGE_EVENTS`: perform Event-management operations for the applicable
    Unit scope.
  - `SUBMIT_AUDITS`: submit Audits for the applicable Unit scope.
  - `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS`: manage subordinate authorized users and their
    permitted assignments.
- Root-level organization configuration is not an ordinary MVP permission;
  Root-specific configuration is reserved to the RootUnit owner.

### Permission Scope

Each ordinary scoped permission grant has exactly one conceptual scope:

- `SELF`: the exact assigned Unit only.
- `SELF_AND_CHILDREN`: the assigned Unit and its immediate children.
- `SELF_AND_DESCENDANTS`: the assigned Unit and its complete subtree.

`MANAGE_STRUCTURE` does not use this ordinary scope model. Its Unit is a
structural anchor, and the grant controls eligible structural operations in
the hierarchy below that anchor according to the approved structural-anchor
rules.

The architecture must preserve the distinction between the membership Unit,
the permission grant, the structural anchor where applicable, the target Unit,
and the effective scope used during authorization.

### Delegation and Local Hierarchy

- A User may not grant a permission they do not possess.
- A delegated grant must target a Unit within the delegator's effective
  authority and a subordinate recipient.
- For ordinary scoped grants, delegated scope must be strictly narrower than
  the delegator's effective scope for that permission:
  - `SELF` cannot delegate that permission further.
  - `SELF_AND_CHILDREN` may delegate `SELF`.
  - `SELF_AND_DESCENDANTS` may delegate `SELF` or `SELF_AND_CHILDREN`, but
    not `SELF_AND_DESCENDANTS`.
- `MANAGE_STRUCTURE` does not use ordinary scopes; its delegated structural
  anchor must be a strict descendant of the delegator's structural anchor.
- Each Unit has a local numeric authority level for its authorized users.
- Every Commander has the unique level-0 authorized-user membership for the
  Unit; all non-Commanders have levels >= 1, and equal nonzero levels are
  allowed.
- The numeric hierarchy is local to each Unit, not a global RootUnit ranking.
- Equal-authority users may not administratively modify or remove one
  another.
- Authority comparison first considers Unit ancestry and effective scope,
  then local authority level when users operate at the same Unit.
- Ancestor authority is structurally above descendant authority when the
  applicable permission scope covers the target Unit.

### Public Access and Server-Side Security

- Public read-only Unit information requires no authentication.
- Protected writes and all authority checks are enforced server-side.
- Frontend visibility is never the security boundary.

### Unit Deletion

- MVP hard deletion is leaf-only and requires structural authority anchored at
  an ancestor of the target.
- Submitted Audits and preserved roster/membership history block deletion.
- Draft Audits must be explicitly removed or resolved before deletion and may
  not be silently cascaded.
- Permanent or historical EventParticipation records must not be silently
  destroyed; exact protected states are deferred to EventParticipation
  architecture.
- Post-MVP archival and administrator-reviewed RootUnit deletion are not to
  be designed in full here; archival is the preferred future direction.

## Required Design Questions

The architecture agent answered or explicitly routed the following. It
recommended schema, cardinality, constraint, and module approaches, but this
ticket remained design-only and RED until human approval.

### A. RootUnit and Commander Representation

Determine the smallest clean representation for:

- RootUnit designation and the invariant that every Unit belongs to one
  RootUnit tree;
- exactly one Commander per Unit;
- RootUnit owner/Commander identity;
- child Unit Commander identity;
- already-authenticated User requirements;
- manual RootUnit bootstrap and administrator ownership assignment;
- manual RootUnit ownership transfer;
- Commander replacement by an ancestor Commander only;
- foreign-key behavior, deletion behavior, and transaction boundaries.

Evaluate whether ownership should be represented directly, through a
dedicated relationship, or by another minimal approach. Explain which
relationships are product concepts versus implementation conveniences.

### B. Authorized-User and Permission Representation

Determine the minimum representation for independent assignments containing
User, Unit, Permission, Scope, and local authority level. Address:

- the seven MVP permissions and their exact conceptual targets;
- duplicate assignment prevention;
- multiple assignments for one User at different Units;
- direct versus inherited authority;
- grant, update, revoke, and replacement behavior;
- whether Commander status is stored separately from permissions;
- how RootUnit-owner-only configuration is kept outside ordinary permissions;
- whether a separate authorization module is justified.

Do not introduce generalized enterprise RBAC or speculative role systems.

### C. Scope and Effective Permission Resolution

Define the conceptual resolution algorithm or rules for ordinary scoped
permissions using `SELF`, `SELF_AND_CHILDREN`, and `SELF_AND_DESCENDANTS`.
`MANAGE_STRUCTURE` uses its Unit as a structural anchor instead of those
scopes. The design must address:

- target Unit ancestry;
- direct and inherited assignments;
- additive permissions;
- authority isolation between RootUnit trees;
- missing, deleted, moved, or changed Units;
- denial behavior when no applicable assignment exists;
- precedence when multiple assignments apply;
- how ancestry precedence and local authority level are compared.

The design must show how the algorithm supports the concrete user stories in
this ticket without turning implementation details into accepted decisions.

### D. Delegation Ceiling and Authorized-User Administration

Define server-side rules for `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS`, including:

- delegator possession of the permission;
- target Unit coverage;
- strictly narrower delegated scope;
- subordinate recipient requirement;
- local authority-level assignment and updates;
- equal-authority peer protection;
- ancestor-versus-descendant precedence;
- removal of one assignment without removing independent assignments;
- denial of Commander removal or replacement by ordinary authorized users;
- auditability and transaction behavior for grants, changes, and revocations.

### E. Commander Assignment and Replacement

Define the distinct hierarchy operation for:

- designating a Commander for a new child Unit;
- requiring the intended Commander to correspond to an existing Auth.js
  User;
- adding the intended Commander as an authorized user when required;
- determining the ancestor Commander and verifying that the acting User has
  applicable `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS` authority covering the target Unit;
- replacing a descendant Commander;
- preventing ordinary authorized users, descendant peers, and users lacking
  the required combined authority from replacing a Commander;
- RootUnit owner transfer by website administrator only and RootUnit
  Commander continuity.

### F. Unit Structure, Movement, and Deletion

Define the conceptual semantics of `MANAGE_UNIT` versus structural-anchor
`MANAGE_STRUCTURE`, including:

- metadata editing versus create, move, restructure, and eligible delete;
- authorization to create a child and establish its Commander in one atomic
  operation;
- rejection of any operation that would leave a valid Unit without exactly
  one Commander;
- prevention of cycles, cross-RootUnit moves, and invalid ancestry;
- descendant movement as one coherent operation;
- recalculation of inherited authority after a move;
- preservation of direct assignments on moved Units;
- historical dependencies that block hard deletion;
- transaction, concurrency, and failure behavior for structural changes.

### G. Module Ownership and Future Boundaries

Recommend:

- which authority behavior belongs to the Units module;
- whether a separate authorization module is needed;
- what authentication infrastructure remains shared;
- what semantic capability Events, rosters, and Audits may eventually need;
- how future RootUnit-scoped ranks, medals, and configuration can use the
  RootUnit boundary without being implemented now.

For every proposed cross-module capability, identify a real consumer,
producer, minimum semantics, and the point at which it should be formalized.
Do not create speculative contracts.

### H. Product Boundary Questions

Explicitly identify any remaining product decision rather than silently
choosing one. The following decisions are established and must not be
reopened:

- RootUnit ownership transfer is website-administrator-only;
- Unit creation and initial Commander assignment are atomic;
- Commander status grants no additional MVP operational permissions;
- descendant Commander replacement requires both ancestor Commander status
  and applicable `MANAGE_AUTHORIZED_USERS` authority covering the target Unit.

specific distinction not determined by the requirements must be surfaced
The exact EventParticipation lifecycle states that qualify as protected
historical records are intentionally deferred to future EventParticipation
architecture. Any other operation-specific distinction not determined by the
requirements must be surfaced rather than invented.

Technical choices such as table names, foreign-key actions, indexes, query
shape, caching, and transaction implementation are for the architecture
agent to resolve and recommend, not product questions unless they change
observable behavior.

## User Stories / Design Validation Examples

The architecture must walk through these concrete behaviors:

- **US-AUTH-01:** `MANAGE_ROSTER @ Battalion / SELF` permits Battalion
  roster work, not Company work, and cannot be delegated.
- **US-AUTH-02:** `MANAGE_ROSTER @ Regiment / SELF_AND_CHILDREN` covers the
  Regiment and immediate Battalions, not Companies, and may delegate `SELF`.
- **US-AUTH-03:** `MANAGE_EVENTS @ RootUnit / SELF_AND_DESCENDANTS` covers
  the organization but not another RootUnit and cannot delegate the same
  scope.
- **US-AUTH-04:** A delegator cannot grant equal or broader scope.
- **US-AUTH-05:** Equal-level authorized users cannot modify or remove one
  another but may manage a lower-level subordinate when permitted.
- **US-AUTH-06:** Applicable ancestor authority outranks descendant local
  authority.
- **US-AUTH-07:** Ordinary authorized users cannot remove or replace a
  Commander; only an ancestor Commander may replace a descendant Commander.
- **US-AUTH-08:** Commander status does not grant missing operational
  permissions.
- **US-AUTH-09:** Metadata authority and structure authority remain separate.
- **US-AUTH-10:** Moving a Unit removes old inherited authority, applies new
  inherited authority, and preserves direct assignments.
- **US-AUTH-11:** Revoking one assignment does not remove independent
  assignments.
- **US-AUTH-12:** No authority or configuration crosses RootUnit trees.
- **US-AUTH-13:** Hard deletion is denied when protected history would be
  destroyed.
- **US-AUTH-14:** A website administrator establishes a RootUnit and assigns
  an already-authenticated User as owner/Commander.
- **US-AUTH-15:** RootUnit remains the future boundary for organization-wide
  ranks and medals without implementing those features.
